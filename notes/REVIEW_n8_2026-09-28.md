## What I checked and found correct
**Mathematics**
- Reduction: thm:constrank gives rank 6 and s_a = 2. b ↦ B_b is injective, and dim P_a(W) = 8 − rk β(a,·) = s_a.
- The "Consequently" statement: with s_a = 2 and no bent component, lem:count gives Σ_b(2^{8−rk} − 1) = 765, with every term ≥ 3 (a term with B_b = 0 contributes 255). So all ranks are 6.
- Lemma closure, including (P1), (P2) and (P3).
- Invariance: B^g is a right action, and P_a(W^g) = P_{ga}(W)^g. So H = Stab(U) ∩ Stab(r) preserves admissible cosets and candidates.
- The induction that every solution is equivalent to one containing an unpruned node of each dimension.
- J = bits 0, 13, 22 = 4202497, with radical <e6, e7>.

**Code**
- Canonical coset representatives: a fully reduced echelon basis, zero at the pivots; pext/pdep over the non-pivot mask is a bijection.
- make_child: y + W' is admissible iff y + W and y + c + W are both in C(W) and no new full point meets it. Both representatives are handled, with dedup by bitset. The hash sets at d ≥ 4 are correct: nonzero keys, load ≤ 1/4, build and clear paired.
- points_child, reduce8, in_span: correct Gaussian reduction, since leading bits are distinct and the basis is kept sorted.
- choose_point: P2 and P3 as stated.
- expand: BFS orbits with the transversal u[iy] = u[x]·g_k (right action). The random-product and Schreier stabiliser elements are correct, and they are checked at runtime.
- stab_point transversal.
- Level-1 generators fix J.
- --part: the RNG is reset from the path at every child, so both parts see the same level-3 list. I confirmed this by hash.
- Integer widths: 28-bit masks, bitsets of 2^(28−d) bits plus one word, int indices for m ≤ 740,736.

**Validation evidence**
- Certificate check (verify_cert.py): complete and correct for levels 1–2: exact candidate sets from the definitions, invertibility, stabilisation, fixing R, and the mapping. The 486 roots match as subspaces.
- Pfaffian table: correct method, and the count agrees with the theoretical 149,920,960.

**n = 6.**
- All 12,288 nosym W carry one of the two closed-pair invariants: 192 have 210 closed pairs, 12,096 have 50.
- I computed |Aut(W)| directly by backtracking: 362,880 (= |ΓL(3,4)|; GL(3,4) stabilises the x^5 component space, 300 of 300 random elements) and 5,760.
- Orbit–stabiliser then predicts |GL(6,2)| · 63 / 18228 · (1/362880 + 1/5760) = 192 + 12,096 = 12,288, exactly the nosym count.
- 400 random GL-conjugates of the symmetric-run solutions, normalised to contain J, are all in the nosym list.

This is a stronger n = 6 validation than the pending brute force; I recommend putting the orbit–stabiliser identity in the paper.
